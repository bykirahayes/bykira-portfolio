How much should a small-business website cost?

There is no useful single price for “a website”. A fair quote depends on what the website must achieve, how much needs creating, and how much responsibility the developer is taking on.

What shapes the quote

The page count is only one part of the work. A focused landing page can still require careful messaging, design and testing. A larger site may be straightforward when its content and structure are already clear.

The main cost drivers are usually discovery, content planning, copy support, visual design, responsive development, custom functionality, integrations, accessibility checks, testing and launch support. Ask whether those jobs are included rather than comparing totals alone.

Content readiness matters

If photography, brand assets and approved copy are ready, a project can move quickly. If the website must also clarify the offer and organise years of existing material, that thinking should be recognised in the scope.

How to compare proposals fairly

A good proposal should state the pages or templates being created, the number of revision stages, who supplies content, what happens at launch, and what is outside the agreed work. It should also explain payment stages and ownership.

Useful question

“What would cause this price or timeline to change?” A clear answer tells you more than a long feature list.

The cheapest quote is not automatically poor and the highest is not automatically best. Look for evidence that the person understands your audience, priorities and practical constraints.

Setting a useful budget

Start with the business problem rather than a list of effects. Explain what visitors need to understand, trust and do. Then decide what you can comfortably invest to solve that problem. Sharing an honest range helps a developer propose the right-sized approach instead of guessing.

Protect a little budget for content, photography, domains or third-party services when those are not included. A responsible proposal will make recurring costs visible before you approve it.
